# Customer Shopping Behavior Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer shopping behavior using transactional data from 3,900 purchases across different product categories. The objective is to identify spending patterns, customer segments, product preferences, and subscription behavior to support data-driven business decisions.

The analysis combines Python for data cleaning and exploratory data analysis, PostgreSQL for business-focused SQL analysis, and Power BI for interactive dashboard visualization.

## Business Problem

A retail company wants to better understand its customers' shopping behavior to improve sales, customer satisfaction, and long-term loyalty.

The project investigates how customer demographics, product categories, discounts, reviews, shipping preferences, and subscription status relate to purchasing behavior.

The primary business question is:

How can the company leverage consumer shopping data to identify trends, improve customer engagement, and optimize marketing and product strategies?

## Dataset

- Dataset: Customer Shopping Behavior
- Records: 3,900
- Columns: 18
- File: `customer_shopping_behavior.csv`

The dataset contains information about customer demographics, purchases, product categories, payment-related preferences, discounts, reviews, shipping types, and previous purchase history.

Key features include:

- Customer demographics: Age, Gender, Location
- Purchase details: Item Purchased, Category, Purchase Amount
- Shopping preferences: Size, Color, Season
- Customer behavior: Previous Purchases, Frequency of Purchases
- Marketing factors: Discount Applied, Promo Code Used, Subscription Status
- Customer feedback: Review Rating
- Delivery preferences: Shipping Type

Note: The dataset contains 37 missing values in the Review Rating column, which were handled during data cleaning.

## Project Workflow

### 1. Data Loading and Exploration

- Loaded the dataset into Python using Pandas.
- Examined the dataset structure using `df.info()`.
- Generated descriptive statistics using `df.describe()`.
- Reviewed data types, missing values, and summary statistics.

### 2. Data Cleaning and Feature Engineering

- Checked for missing values in the dataset.
- Imputed missing review ratings using the median rating of the corresponding product category.
- Renamed columns to snake_case for consistency and readability.
- Created an `age_group` column by grouping customers into age categories.
- Created a `purchase_frequency_days` column from purchase frequency information.
- Checked whether `discount_applied` and `promo_code_used` were redundant and removed `promo_code_used`.
- Connected Python to PostgreSQL and loaded the cleaned DataFrame into the database for further analysis.

### 3. SQL Analysis Using PostgreSQL

The project answers 10 business questions using SQL queries.

1. Revenue by Gender

   Compared total revenue generated by male and female customers.

2. High-Spending Customers Using Discounts

   Identified customers who used discounts but spent at least the average purchase amount.

3. Top 5 Products by Average Review Rating

   Ranked products based on their average customer review ratings.

4. Standard vs. Express Shipping

   Compared the average purchase amounts for Standard and Express shipping.

5. Subscribers vs. Non-Subscribers

   Compared customer counts, average spending, and total revenue based on subscription status.

6. Products with the Highest Discount Rates

   Identified the five products with the highest percentage of purchases involving discounts.

7. Customer Segmentation

   Classified customers into three segments based on previous purchases:
   - New: 1 previous purchase
   - Returning: 2–10 previous purchases
   - Loyal: More than 10 previous purchases

8. Top 3 Products Within Each Category

   Used Common Table Expressions (CTEs) and the `ROW_NUMBER()` window function to rank the most purchased products within each category.

9. Repeat Buyers and Subscription Status

   Examined the subscription status of customers with more than five previous purchases.

10. Revenue by Age Group

    Calculated and compared total revenue across different customer age groups.

## Key Findings

The following findings are based on the SQL query results documented in the project report.

- Revenue by gender: Male customers generated 157,890 in revenue, while female customers generated 75,191.
- Product ratings: Gloves had the highest average review rating at 3.86, followed by Sandals at 3.84 and Boots at 3.82.
- Shipping comparison: Express shipping customers had an average purchase amount of 60.48, compared with 58.46 for Standard shipping.
- Subscription analysis: Subscribers had an average spend of 59.49, while non-subscribers had an average spend of 59.87. Non-subscribers generated higher total revenue, partly because they represented a larger customer group.
- Discount usage: Hats had the highest discount rate at 50.00%, followed by Sneakers at 49.66% and Coats at 49.07%.
- Customer segmentation: The analysis classified 3,116 customers as Loyal, 701 as Returning, and 83 as New.
- Repeat buyers: Among customers with more than five previous purchases, 958 were subscribers and 2,518 were non-subscribers.
- Revenue by age group: Young Adults generated the highest revenue at 62,143, followed by Middle-aged customers at 59,197, Adults at 55,978, and Seniors at 55,763.

Note: Revenue values are expressed in the dataset's purchase currency, USD. The findings describe the recorded transactions and do not by themselves establish causation.

## How to Run the Project

1. Download the dataset and save it as `customer_shopping_behavior.csv`.
2. Open JupyterLab and run the notebook `Customer_Shopping_Behaviour_Analysis.ipynb`.
3. Install the required Python libraries, including Pandas and the appropriate PostgreSQL database connector.
4. Execute the data loading, cleaning, and feature engineering steps.
5. Configure your PostgreSQL connection and load the cleaned DataFrame into PostgreSQL.
6. Open pgAdmin 4 and execute the SQL queries against the customer table.
7. Open the Power BI dashboard file in Power BI Desktop to explore the visualizations.

Database credentials should be configured locally and must not be committed to GitHub.
